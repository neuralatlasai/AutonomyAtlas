\section{Verification whose errors are measured independently}\label{sec:C34:04}
Agent progress depends heavily on the quality of evaluators. A verifier can miss a requirement, reward a shortcut, crash selectively, or share the executor's mistaken interpretation. Increasing the number of judges does not guarantee independent evidence. The research target is to quantify the conditions under which a verifier's acceptance predicts the intended outcome and to detect when those conditions change.

TestJack's adversarial auditing and AgentHorizon-style evaluator inspection motivate a useful separation between an artifact's tested behavior, specification interpretation, and grading implementation~\cite{yao2026testjack,corpus261011050}. A witness test that fails a candidate and passes a reference is evidence of a difference; whether that difference violates the task still depends on the requirement. Reference agreement alone is not a complete oracle.

A discriminating verifier experiment inserts known fault classes into artifacts while preserving plausible surface quality. Vary the fault's visibility, execution path, and relationship to the written specification. Compare deterministic checks, model judges, adversarial test generation, and combinations. Report false acceptance, false rejection, abstention, cost, and fault-family transfer. Keep the final fault families hidden from verifier optimization. If the verifier is updated, independently reevaluate its errors before using it to select a stronger executor.

The unresolved problem is adaptive evaluator exploitation. A static false-acceptance estimate may not apply after an improver repeatedly searches for artifacts that the verifier accepts. Evaluation should therefore include an adaptive attacker constrained to the same information and resources available to the optimizer, followed by an independent audit. This does not produce a universal guarantee, but it tests the distribution shift induced by the very process the verifier is meant to supervise.

Statistical validity and semantic validity must be checked separately. REUSE controls erroneous promotions on its declared population under an independent initial sample and restricted decision-only feedback~\cite{ch02_reuse2026}. That guarantee concerns the supplied score; it cannot establish that the score measures the complete specification. Conversely, a semantically strong test set can still yield invalid statistical comparisons after unrestricted adaptive reuse. A credible improvement claim needs both a justified outcome definition and an evaluation protocol valid for the optimizer's actual information access.
