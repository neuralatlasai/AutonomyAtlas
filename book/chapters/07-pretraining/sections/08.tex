\section{Experiments linking infrastructure to behavior}
\label{sec:C07:experiments}
A scientific training study should identify the intervention and preserve the other important determinants. To test data mixture, hold architecture, token budget, optimizer, and evaluation interface fixed while changing a documented mixture weight. To test parallelism, compare time to the same loss and downstream quality under matched numerical settings, while reporting any unavoidable batch or precision differences. To test long-context pretraining, include tasks requiring early constraints, distant evidence integration, and resumed execution rather than relying only on a nominal context-length setting.

Implementation ablations should have interpretable predictions. Enabling optimizer partitioning should reduce persistent-state residency according to the specified bytes-per-parameter model, while potentially increasing communication. Activation checkpointing should reduce retained activation memory and increase executed work. Packing should increase utilization without allowing cross-example visibility when independence is intended. These predictions can be checked with tensors and traces before a costly full run. A failed prediction indicates an accounting or implementation error that should be resolved before interpreting downstream benchmark changes.

The most useful negative results preserve their conditions. If a data addition lowers validation loss but worsens tool reliability, inspect whether the new examples changed serialization conventions or encouraged unsupported operations. If a distributed optimization speeds individual steps but worsens time to target quality, examine batch-size and optimizer effects. If resumption changes behavior, compare restored data position, random state, and parameter partitions before attributing the difference to an inherent instability of learning. Infrastructure and statistical learning are coupled, but their effects can still be separated through targeted measurements.

Pretraining supplies a reusable statistical substrate, not a complete agent operating contract. Its quality depends on the objective, evidence, optimization, and implementation all describing the same causal task. Distributed systems determine what training is feasible and whether its result can be reconstructed; data design determines which conditional behaviors are learned. The subsequent post-training stage can specialize this substrate for reasoning and tools, but cannot automatically repair evidence that was never recorded or an execution interface whose semantics remain undefined.

One final integration check should compare a restored checkpoint's logits with the saved run on a fixed, permission-cleared diagnostic batch before resumed optimization. This detects missing adapters, incompatible tokenizer artifacts, or incorrect parameter reconstruction before new updates obscure the difference. Agreement does not prove complete replay, but disagreement immediately falsifies it.

\subsection{Predictive supervision inside policy optimization}
\label{sec:C07:worldcotraining}
The boundary between predictive training and interactive optimization is visible in PaW. Its current second version uses action-conditioned next-observation targets from policy rollouts, selects transitions by sampled action surprisal, and combines a confidence-clipped observation loss with the policy loss. Action and observation masks allow both terms in a causal forward pass; the deployed agent does not need an extra imagined-observation call~\cite{rewrite_paw2026}. This is auxiliary supervision during policy optimization, not evidence that ordinary corpus pretraining and environment interaction are interchangeable.

For a target-token probability $p$, the clipped auxiliary term is $(1-p)\mathbf1[p\leq\rho]$, with threshold $\rho\in[0,1]$ and a detached indicator in the checked code. \path{compute_world_model_loss} in \path{verl/trainer/ppo/core_algos.py} shifts the token mask and keeps the full observation-mask denominator; \path{DataParallelPPOActor.update_policy} combines it with the policy loss~\cite{rewrite_paw_repo}. Renormalizing only by surviving confidence-selected tokens would change this objective. One forward pass also does not imply zero extra training work: appended observation positions require activations and projections even when their eventual loss mask is sparse.

A first-order calculation exposes possible co-training conflict. Let $g_\pi=\nabla L_\pi$ and $g_w=\nabla L_w$ at the current parameters, treating selection and auxiliary weight $\lambda\geq0$ as fixed. Under update $\Delta\theta=-\eta(g_\pi+\lambda g_w)$ and differentiable policy loss,
\begin{equation}
 L_\pi(\theta+\Delta\theta)-L_\pi(\theta)
 =-\eta(\|g_\pi\|^2+\lambda g_\pi^\top g_w)
 +o(\eta).
\end{equation}
A sufficiently negative cross term can increase the policy loss despite decreasing the joint objective. This local deduction motivates measuring gradient interaction and task transfer rather than assuming that better observation prediction is always policy-improving. Its implication is conditional: a descent surrogate and verified environment return need not agree either.

DGS-WM makes selection policy-dependent in a different way. Section 3 scores candidate observation units by the likelihood they assign to the recorded next policy response, retains units above a within-observation threshold, and applies detached confidence weighting to their predictive loss~\cite{rewrite_selectivewm2026}. The full observation remains available to the policy; selection changes auxiliary targets. Response support is a correlational utility proxy. An already mistaken response can favor evidence consistent with that mistake, while facts needed only many steps later can receive weak support from the immediate response. Compare full-observation supervision, random units matched for target tokens, action-surprisal selection, and response-support selection under the same rollout data and complete compute budget.

The spectral composition study supplies a complementary intervention rather than another target-selection rule. It decomposes updates from a common pretrained base, projects policy-update matrices onto or away from left and right singular subspaces of world-model updates, and reevaluates behavior~\cite{rewrite_wmcomposition2026}. Preserving a subspace norm is not equivalent to preserving a function. A sound comparison includes random subspaces of equal rank and norm-matched perturbations, then measures both transition prediction and task return. These controls distinguish a consequential update direction from an attractive representation visualization, while retaining architecture and coordinate-system assumptions in every interpretation.
