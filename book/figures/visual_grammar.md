# Scientific illustration grammar

Use original image-generated illustrations to explain a mechanism, an information boundary, a counterexample, or a proposed experimental intervention. Every figure must answer a specific scientific question tied to the chapter's equations and evidence. A visually complex ecosystem inventory is better expressed as a validated table when its distinctions cannot remain legible in one figure.

The cumulative edition contains 35 original illustrations across all 34 chapters, including two in Chapter 1. The figure plan records actual section bindings, mechanism scope, assets, prompts and print dimensions. The manifest binds accepted assets and generated edit inputs to their hashes; the final validation record states the PDF review scope.

## Appearance and hierarchy

Use a white background, charcoal line work and text, and one restrained orange accent. Orange identifies the figure's declared intervention, active path, or guard; explain its local meaning. It never represents measured superiority, safety, or confidence merely by color. Maintain the same meaning within a figure. The figure must remain understandable in grayscale through labels, line styles and shapes.

Use monoline symbols with consistent strokes and generous negative space. Prefer two or three aligned panels to a dense web of crossings. Document shapes represent records; cylinders represent explicitly labeled stores; circles can represent policies or operators; diamonds represent predicates with labeled outcomes. A key denotes a labeled authorization input. Use an explicit frozen-checkpoint label for fixed parameters rather than a security glyph. Use appropriate mathematical symbols rather than decorative brain imagery, product logos, mascots or hardware photographs. Inspiration from scientific references informs clarity and hierarchy; do not copy or trace their figures, layouts or branding.

Target the actual 124 mm publication width. Body labels should be about 9 pt at final print size and never below 7 pt; panel titles should remain larger. Subscripts, superscripts, arrows, minus signs and Greek letters require individual inspection at that size. Use restrained stroke weights that remain visible after print reduction, with at least 300 effective pixels per inch. Enlarge or split an overloaded figure instead of shrinking its labels. Keep text black or charcoal; orange is primarily a structural accent.

## Symbols and arrows

Bind every symbol to the chapter's local notation before generation. Distinguish history `H`, context `C`, memory `M`, environment state `E`, policy `pi`, weights `theta`, version manifest `nu`, current authority epoch `alpha`, operation identity `k`, and effect ledger `lambda` whenever those objects appear. The plan's proposed symbols are not automatic additions to the manuscript notation. Do not conflate a session, checkpoint, KV cache, memory service, skill, protocol request or external effect.

Define each arrow type in its panel or caption:

- Solid directed arrows show the named data transformation, control transition, executed operation or observed evidence flow. A figure using several meanings must label them separately.
- Dashed directed arrows show hypothetical predictions, proposed interventions or another explicitly named relationship. They never silently turn a prediction into an observation.
- Plain connectors show dependency, correspondence or provenance; they do not imply causation or execution order.
- Guarded branches show predicate outcomes with rejection, denial or unresolved states when scientifically relevant. A successful guard establishes only its declared property.
- Feedback arrows state what information returns and which component may use it. Report-only or sealed evaluation has no feedback path into training or selection.

Avoid ambiguous bidirectional arrows and unexplained crossing lines. Do not imply that authorization proves task correctness, that a receipt proves durability, or that a local journal makes external effects atomic.

## Evidence, generation and acceptance

Label the visual's scope as a conceptual mechanism, analytical construction, or proposed experimental design. A generated illustration must not contain invented benchmark points, latency curves, confidence intervals, calibration plots, rankings or measured proportions. Exact numerical evidence belongs in validated LaTeX tables with source version, population, denominator, units and uncertainty. Equations or symbolic inequalities may appear when their assumptions are defined in the text and every symbol can be checked.

Create and revise scientific assets with image-generation models only. Do not author them with plotting, diagram-generation, SVG, TikZ, Mermaid, Python or other code-to-image methods. PDF rasterization and contact sheets are permitted solely for layout inspection. Record the actual tool and model identifier when exposed; do not claim an unavailable requested model version. Preserve prompts, accepted asset hashes, attribution and computational-assistance disclosure. Record source-inspired adaptations and check applicable permissions before using protected material.

Before acceptance, inspect every label, glyph, arrowhead, branch, connector and caption against the equations and source-supported mechanism. Render the actual LaTeX placement and verify print-size legibility, crop, alignment, grayscale interpretation, figure number and cross-reference. Confirm that no implicit edge, decorative symbol or simplified panel overstates the scientific claim. An asset's existence is not proof of semantic or publication acceptance.
